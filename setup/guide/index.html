Filling in the sake setup form

A working guide for whoever is entering the data. Read it once, then keep it open in a tab.

OneWhat you are doing

You are describing each sake you carry so it can go into the SakeYō app: the catalog people search, and the engine that recommends bottles and matches them to food.

Two things follow from that. Accuracy beats completeness — a wrong number quietly sends the wrong sake to the wrong drinker, and nobody can see that it happened. And a few specific numbers do a lot of work, which is why the form pushes on them.

The one rule that matters: if you do not know it, leave it blank. A blank is a job someone can finish later. A guess that looks like a fact is worse than nothing, because no one knows to check it.

Nothing you enter is published anywhere. It comes to us as a file, we check it, and it goes live only after that.

TwoBefore you start

  • Use the link we sent you. It looks like sakeyo.com/setup/?importer=yourname and it opens with your range already filled in from your own website — names, breweries, prices, photos. You are checking and completing it, not typing it from scratch.
  • One entry per bottle size. A sake sold in 720ml and 1800ml is two entries. Same sake, different product, different price, different page.
  • Your work saves as you type, in this browser, on this computer. There is no login and no send button until you are finished.
  • It does not follow you around. A different computer, a different browser, or a cleared history means starting again. If you need to move or hand over, use Download → JSON file, then Open a saved file on the other machine.

Work through one bottle at a time. Finishing ten properly is worth more than starting fifty.

ThreeThe bottle

The starred fields are the ones we cannot list a bottle without.

  • Name — in rōmaji, as you sell it. Include the grade if it is part of the name people see. Leave out the bottle size and any gift-box wording.
  • Name in Japanese — copy it off the label if you can. It is how we match your bottle to the same sake from another source, so it is worth the effort.
  • Brewery — the kura, not the brand. Tenzan Sake Brewery, not Shichida.
  • Brand or series — the label family, if there is one. Shichida. Kurouemon. Stella.
  • Grade — junmai daiginjo, junmai ginjo, honjōzō and so on. If the ingredients list shows brewing alcohol, it is not a junmai. See reading the label.
  • Size — in millilitres. 720, 1800, 300.
  • Alcohol — the percentage. 15.5, not 15.5% and not 15–16. If the label says 14度, that is 14.
  • Product page — the page on your own site for this exact size. When the marketplace opens, this is where a buyer gets sent, so a link to the wrong size sells the wrong bottle.

FourThe photo

Link to an image on your site, or upload one. Either is fine.

What we need from it:

  • The whole bottle, cap to base. A close-up of the label alone cannot be used.
  • Upright, straight on, in focus.
  • A plain background — white is ideal, a transparent PNG is better still.
  • 1000 pixels tall or more.
  • No watermark, no price sticker, no hand holding it, one bottle only.

Do not try to cut the bottle out of its background yourself. We do that, and our tool does it better than editing by hand. Send the clean original.

FiveThe specs

Four numbers decide where a sake sits in the app: polishing ratio, SMV, acidity, and alcohol. Get those and everything downstream works. Miss one and the bottle needs judging by taste instead — see the next section.

FieldWhat it isWhere to find it
Polishing ratioHow much of the rice grain is left. 60% means 40% was milled away. Lower means more polished.On the back label, almost always.
SMVNihonshu-do. Roughly dry (positive) to sweet (negative). Keep the sign: +3, -1.5.Sometimes on the label. Often only on the kura's spec sheet.

SMV and acidity are the two most likely to be missing from a label, and the two most worth one email to the brewery. If you are already asking them something, ask for those — and for the yeast while you are there.

Four of these feed the taste scales. Polishing ratio and yeast set fruity/savoury between them; alcohol sets body; SMV and acidity together set dry/sweet, and acidity sets the acidity scale on its own. A missing yeast does not stop us working out fruity/savoury, but it can move the answer by a point.

SixThe taste scales

Under the specs are four scales: dry/sweet, acidity, body, and fruity/savoury. They are how the app matches a sake to a person and to a dish.

If you filled in all four specs, the form works these out for you and marks them from your specs. There is nothing to do. Leave them alone unless you have drunk the sake and genuinely disagree.

If a spec is missing, the form says so and asks you to set all four by taste.

Why all four, when only one spec is missing? The four are not independent — they are read together. Three calculated scores next to one judged score looks measured from the outside, and no one downstream can tell which is which. So it is all four by taste, or all four from specs.

Setting them by taste is a real answer, not a fallback. A bottle with no scores at all never reaches a recommendation or a food pairing — it sits in the catalog and is never suggested to anyone. If your team knows the sake, say so on the scales.

Read the scales like this:

  • Dry / sweet — 1 is sweet, 5 is bone dry.
  • Acidity — 1 is soft and round, 5 is sharp and lifted.
  • Body — 1 is light and watery-clean, 5 is thick and full.
  • Fruity / savoury — 1 is fruit and flowers, 5 is rice, earth and umami.

If nobody on your team has drunk it, leave the scales alone and tell us. Do not invent a profile.

SevenStyle and flags

These change how the sake is described and how it scores, so set them where they apply.

The three menus — starter, pasteurisation, clarity — cover kimoto, yamahai, bodai-moto, nama and nigori. Set them there and do not repeat them in the buttons below.

The buttons cover the rest: genshu, muroka, sparkling, koshu, taruzake, dessert and sour. Turn on what applies.

If you are not sure which starter or pressing method was used, choose Not sure. That is an honest answer and it is better than a wrong one.

EightServing, food, description, price

  • Serving temperature — tap once for good, twice for best. Skip any you are unsure of.
  • Food pairings — be specific. “Grilled mackerel” is useful; “Japanese food” is not.
  • Tasting notes — what it actually tastes of. Skip it rather than writing marketing lines.
  • Description — your words about the sake. We may shorten it.
  • Retail price — the shelf price for this size, with the currency. Trade price is never shown publicly.
  • Availability — be honest about allocation and seasonal bottles. It saves everyone a disappointed customer.

NineThe brewery

The brewery block at the bottom is filled in once per brewery, not once per bottle. Do it on the first bottle from that kura and it carries across the rest.

Founding year, town and house style are the useful ones. They are what a drinker reads on the brewery page.

ElevenWhen a bottle is done

You do not have to judge this. The strip at the top of each bottle tells you.

Ready to set up. We have everything we need for this bottle.

Nearly there. We still need bottle size, alcohol and the flavour profile.

It names exactly what is missing. The counter in the top right — 8 / 22 ready — is the same test across your whole range, and the dot beside each bottle in the list shows it at a glance.

Aim to get every bottle green. Where you cannot, send it anyway and tell us what is missing and why.

TwelveSending it back

Click Download. You get three choices:

  • JSON file — send us this one. It is what we load, and it carries any photos you uploaded.
  • Excel workbook — take this if you want to read it over, circulate it internally, or keep a copy. Spreadsheets cannot carry photos.
  • CSV — the same rows as plain text.

Email it to brad@sakesho.app. If the file is too big for email, a shared link is fine.

Sending a partial list is fine and often better. We can load what is ready and come back for the rest.

ThirteenCommon mistakes

  • Polishing ratio upside down. 60% means 60% of the grain is left. A daiginjo is 50% or lower, never 80%.
  • A dropped minus sign. SMV −1.5 and +1.5 are opposite sakes.
  • One entry for two sizes. The 720 and the 1800 are separate entries with separate pages and prices.
  • A product page for the range. Link to the exact bottle, not a category listing.
  • Brand in the brewery box. The brewery is the company that made it.
  • Units typed into numbers. 720, not 720ml. 15.5, not 15.5%.
  • Filling gaps with plausible numbers. If the spec sheet does not have it, leave it blank and tell us.
  • Working in two browsers. The two copies do not merge; the second one you send overwrites the first.

Anything unclear, or the form doing something odd — write to brad@sakesho.app and say what you were doing at the time.
